# Design Direction Brief

## Why this comes first

Without a stated direction, generated design converges on the same defaults every time: a centred hero, a purple-to-blue gradient, three feature cards with outline icons, a rounded sans-serif. It is recognisable on sight and it reads as unconsidered. The direction is what prevents that, and it must be chosen BEFORE any markup is written.

## Workflow

1. Write the brief in four lines.
   - **Audience:** who reads this, in what state of mind.
   - **Feeling:** three adjectives, and three the design must NOT be. The negative list does more work than the positive one.
   - **Reference:** a named movement or era, not "modern" or "clean" — those are not directions.
   - **Constraint:** one rule that forces a decision. "No gradients." "Exactly two typefaces." "The grid is visible."
   - **Done when:** all four lines are written and the reference names something specific.

2. Pick the reference from an actual vocabulary.
   - Swiss/International (grid, Helvetica-lineage, flush-left, generous whitespace), Brutalist (raw structure, system fonts, hard edges, visible borders), Editorial (serif display, strong hierarchy, wide measure columns), Bauhaus (primary colours, geometric forms), Memphis (asymmetry, pattern, clashing colour), Japanese minimalism (asymmetric balance, negative space, restrained palette), Art Deco (symmetry, metallic accents, high-contrast geometry), Terminal/technical (monospace, fixed grid, minimal chrome).
   - **Done when:** one is named and the others are consciously rejected.

3. Derive the system from the direction, not from habit.
   - Type scale, spacing scale, palette and border treatment all follow from the reference. Swiss does not get rounded corners. Brutalist does not get soft shadows.
   - **Done when:** each system decision can be traced to the reference.

4. Check the negative list before finishing.
   - Read the three "must not be" adjectives and look at the result. If any of them fits, the direction was not followed.
   - **Done when:** none of the negative adjectives applies.

## The default trap

If the output has a centred hero with a gradient headline, three equal cards below it, and rounded everything — the brief was skipped. Go back to step one.

## Verification

- The brief exists in writing.
- Every system decision traces to the named reference.
- None of the three negative adjectives describes the result.
